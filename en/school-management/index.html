KursOS: lesson schedule, collections and teacher earnings on one record

KursOS is a browser-based, multi-tenant school management system for art schools, music schools, language courses and hobby academies. Recurring lesson series, a five-view calendar, studio and room capacity, student packages with remaining-lesson tracking and waitlists are kept on the same record. The student and parent portal, collections, outstanding balances and teacher earnings under three models run from the same record too. The system was written around the daily work of an art school; each deployment carries the institution's own brand and domain.

What is a school management system?

A school management system is software that keeps an educational institution's timetable, student records and lesson packages, teacher assignments, room usage, collections and teacher payments in a single database. In art and music schools the distinctive requirements are the recurring weekly lesson series, room-type requirements and the number of lessons deducted from a package. KursOS is a multi-tenant school management system that delivers this structure under the institution's own brand.

Who it is for

  • Art schools and studios: painting, ceramics, sculpture and design studios; capped groups, room-type requirements, term packages.
  • Music schools and conservatoire preparation: one-to-one lessons, teacher assignment by instrument, room conflicts.
  • Language courses and tutoring centres: level groups, absences, assignments and parent updates.
  • Sports and hobby academies: recurring weekly timetable, trial lessons, waitlists.

What it does

Calendar and recurring lesson series

A lesson is defined as a series: once a week, at the same time, with the same teacher. When you change one day, the system asks for one of three scopes: only this lesson, this and the following ones, or the whole series. The change does not spill beyond the scope you chose.

The calendar has five views: day, week, month, teacher and room. The room view shows a conflict before it is placed; the teacher view shows at a glance whose week is full.

Studios, rooms and capacity

Each service carries its own duration, capacity and required room type. The ceramics studio needs the room with the kiln, the painting studio the one with daylight; the system does not let an unsuitable room be scheduled. Preparation and clean-up time can be defined between lessons, so two studio sessions that fit back to back on paper do not end up not fitting in practice.

In an institution with branches, each branch has its own working hours and holiday calendar. No lesson can be written into closed hours; a branch-specific holiday stops only that branch.

Students, teachers and packages

A student is attached to a lesson package; the number of remaining lessons drops with each completed lesson. For a student who does not turn up, the institution's policy applies: the lesson is forfeited or turns into credit. Teachers see only their own students.

Waitlist and trial lessons

When capacity is full, the enrolment goes onto the waitlist. When a place opens, an offer goes to the next person; if they do not reply in time, the offer passes to the following one. A trial lesson is a separate record type and is not deducted from a package.

Assignments, announcements and the portal

Teachers set assignments and attach files; students upload their submissions. Institutional announcements go to the relevant group. Students and parents see the timetable, remaining lessons, assignments and outstanding balance on their own portal.

Finance: collections, balances and earnings

Payments are recorded and the outstanding balance is visible per student. There is a rule that blocks lesson scheduling when a payment is overdue; the institution can keep it on or off.

Teacher earnings are calculated under one of three models: a fixed amount per lesson, a percentage of collections, or salaried. All three models can be used side by side in the same institution; each teacher is calculated under their own model. Collections, balances and earnings are taken from the same report screen.

The mobile app: four roles, one app

Coordinator, teacher, student and parent all use the same app; whoever signs in gets their own role's screens. The thing a teacher does most in the room — taking the register — is one tap away, with lessons, announcements, assignments and packages on their own tabs. A teacher or parent attached to more than one institution switches between them from a list.

The app has a demo sign-in: one button for each of the four roles opens a sample institution, and the demo data is refreshed to today on every sign-in, so the dates are never stale.

Messaging and reminders

WhatsApp is connected through the institution's own Meta account. Incoming messages are collected in one inbox and handed to a coordinator when needed. Lesson reminders go out through approved templates; the system keeps a ledger of approved templates and lists as warnings any templates a reminder needs but that have not been approved.

The product is generic, the deployment is yours

KursOS is a product name; it is not the name your students and teachers will see. The system was built multi-tenant from the start and no single institution's name is hard-coded anywhere. The deployment carries your brand in three places at once:

Web interfaceYour institution's name, logo and colour. The logo is tested separately on dark and light backgrounds.
DomainYour own domain or subdomain. The student and parent portal lives at the same address.
Mobile appThere is one app, and the choice of institution is inside it: a user who signs in reaches their own institution, and picks from a list if they belong to more than one. Your institution's name, logo and colour appear inside the app, and mobile access and feature settings are managed from your admin panel. The store listing is ours — you do not need to open an Apple or Google developer account.

So the address, the interface and the brand your students see are yours. Several business units can also run under the same institution: an art school and a music department stand with separate timetables, separate teaching staff and separate service lists, while student and payment records are shared.

Tenant isolation is enforced at database level: a query with no tenant returns no data. Permissions are role-based and re-read on the server with every request; when a permission is revoked, it takes effect on the next operation. Who changed what and when remains in the audit log.

Frequently asked questions

Can we import our existing student list?

An existing student list can be imported into KursOS: student, teacher and package lists held in spreadsheets are imported and the column mapping is agreed together. The import shows a preview first: nothing is saved when the file is uploaded — what will happen to each row (new record, duplicate, phone number to be reformatted) is shown on screen, and it is written only once you approve it. We do the first import during deployment; after that an authorised user does it whenever they need to.

Are there group lessons as well as one-to-one lessons?

KursOS has group lessons alongside one-to-one lessons. The same calendar and package structure works for group lessons; capacity and the waitlist come into play for group lessons.

Do parents sign in?

KursOS has a student and parent portal showing the timetable, remaining lessons, assignments and outstanding balance. Portal access is opened by the institution.

Will our students see the name "KursOS"?

Not in the web interface or the portal: the deployment carries your institution's brand, logo and your own domain, and KursOS is only the product name. On mobile there is a single app; it sits in the store under the product name, but once you are inside it, your institution's branding is what you see.

Does the mobile app need separate development?

Neither separate development nor a separate store release is needed. There is a single mobile app: a user who signs in reaches their own institution, and picks from a list if they belong to more than one. The app uses the same API as the web, carries your institution's branding inside it, and its access and feature settings are managed from your admin panel. You do not need to open an Apple or Google developer account.

What does it cost?
